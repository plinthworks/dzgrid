# Cells spanning rows and columns

Written before the code (CLEANROOM). Plan: `docs/phase-3-steps/step9b-spans-blueprint.md`; owner decisions: `step9b-decisions.md`; browser measures: `spikes/s12-spans/README.md`.

## What the user does
Reads a table where a value shared by consecutive rows (a ward, a date, a patient) is written once on a tall cell; where a total spreads over several columns; where headers are grouped ("Patient" above Name, First name, Age); where some rows are one wide line (a group title, a note). Scrolls through long merged runs, sorts, filters, moves with the keyboard, clicks, selects, exports.

## What they observe
- **Identical values merged:** consecutive rows with the same value in a merging column show one tall cell. The label is centred in the cell, as in a spreadsheet; when the merge is so long that its centre is out of view, the label travels with the scroll and stays at the top or bottom edge of the cell's visible part, so the user always knows what the run is. A short merge (a dozen rows) looks exactly like a spreadsheet's.
- **Spans decided by the data:** a cell may cover several rows or columns because the application says so (a total row over three columns, a slot over two hours).
- **Grouped headers:** a group header sits above its columns, on as many levels as the application nests ("Clinique" above "Constantes" above Tension, Pouls, Température). If the user pins or moves one of its columns, the group shows on each side, with its label repeated. A column with fewer groups above it than the deepest one (or none) has its own header drawn taller, up to the group above it (or to the top). Scrolled sideways, a group half out of view is still drawn from its first column; resized or hidden columns widen or narrow their groups. A group header is not a sort button: a click, Enter or Space on it sorts nothing.
- **Full-width rows:** one cell across the whole width, whose text stays readable while scrolling sideways; the selection box stays.
- Merges follow the order on screen: after a sort or a filter, the cells merge again for the rows now side by side. A merge never crosses a page, a pinned edge, or a group of rows.
- The keyboard treats a spanned cell as one cell: one stop, arrows step over it, and moving back returns to the row and column the user came from (as in a spreadsheet).
- Page Up and Page Down inside a cell taller than a page: the focus stays on the tall cell while the row the user aims at moves on by a page, so pressing again eventually leaves the cell (it never sticks); an arrow then leaves from that row. Ctrl+Home, Ctrl+End and Ctrl+Up/Down inside one cell aim at the edge reached in the same way. Arrows, Home and End that stay on the cell (Down at the bottom edge, Left/Right/Home/End on a full-width row) keep the row and column aimed at.
- A click on a tall cell selects the row under the pointer; Space selects the row where the cell starts. Each row keeps its own selection colour, visible through the tall cell.
- In a CSV export, a merged value is written on every row it covers (the file stays real data); an option writes it once, report style.

## What DzGrid does
- Spans are computed in the core from the displayed rows and the visible columns, never from the scroll: the same rows and columns give the same spans, whatever the window. A grid without span rules pays nothing (the span code lives in its own entries, `@dzgrid/core/spans` and `@dzgrid/dom/spans`).
- Merging compares the stable text of the values (what the set filter matches), so `e` and `é` stay apart; blank cells never merge; `merge.within` cuts a run where other columns change (a city merges only inside its region). Sorting by a merged column is the application's choice: the grid never sorts by itself.
- No overlap: a span asked over another is dropped to one cell and said (`span-overlap`); a span cut by a pinned edge or by the end of the rows is said once (`span-clipped`); a bad span (not a whole number, a rule that throws, `merge` together with `rowSpan`/`colSpan` on one column) is `invalid-span`; a bad group (empty, duplicate id) is `invalid-column-group`; span rules without the spans feature are `missing-feature`. A text reader that throws makes its cell a blank (it merges with nothing) and is said once per column (`invalid-span`, `threw`).
- `settings.fullWidth`: a function is the rule; `undefined`, `null` or `false` mean no full-width rows and say nothing (not even `missing-feature`); any other value (`true`, an object, a string) is refused (`invalid-span` at `settings.fullWidth`, `not a function`) and no row is full width. Without the spans feature, any value other than `undefined`, `null` or `false` is `missing-feature`.
- Resizing a column, or the grid sharing flexible widths again, does not recompute the spans (they do not depend on widths) and does not say a clipped span again; pinning, moving, hiding or showing a column, or new column definitions, do.
- With no column visible, there is no cell: no row is full width and no place has an owner.
- Under virtual scrolling, a tall cell lives in the row where it starts, kept mounted while any of its rows is on screen; it is drawn only over the visible part plus a margin (browsers cap an element's height near 33 million pixels, 17.5 million in Firefox; a run may be a million rows). Covered places have no cell of their own; a tall cell is transparent, so each row it covers keeps its own colour (selected, hovered) through it, pinned columns included (the theme paints rows and pinned segments). Hover: the row under the pointer lights up, also over a covered place of a tall cell, never the row the tall cell lives in (a tall cell is an element of its first row, so the browser's `:hover` would light that one): the feature marks the row under the pointer (`data-hover`, cleared when the pointer leaves the body or the row element is reused) and the theme lights `[data-hover]` instead of `:hover` on rows a tall cell meets. Plain grids keep `:hover`. The feature's own CSS names the grid's rows only: a host element carrying `data-full` or `data-spans` changes nothing. Its text is centred in the whole span and, on a long merge, sticks to the top or bottom edge of the visible part (CSS only, no work while scrolling).
- Grouped headers come from `columns`: a definition with `children` is a group (`{ id, header, children }`), nested as deep as wanted; the base keeps only its columns, the spans feature draws the group rows. Each group row holds, per column the column headers' row holds, either the group's cell (a column header with `aria-colindex` of its first column and `aria-colspan`, at the group's first column on screen), or a hidden header repeating the group's name in the group's other columns, or, where no group stands, a hidden header with the column's own name: screen readers that pair a cell with its headers by position (VoiceOver through Chrome, measured in spike S12) then hear the right groups for every cell. A group without `header` shows its id (as a column does). Group rows count in `aria-rowcount`; body rows start after them. A bad group (empty, duplicate id, no id) is said once per set of definitions (`invalid-column-group`) and its columns stay, as if ungrouped.
- Keys over grouped headers: Up from a column header goes to the nearest group above it (skipping levels where that column has none), Up again climbs; Down comes back to the column the user came up from (or, from a group reached otherwise, its first column), then into the body. Left and Right move from group to group along a group row (a column with no group there is not a stop; the two pieces of a split group are two stops), Home and End to the row's first and last group. Page Down and Ctrl+End from a group go into the body under the column aimed at. A focused group is the grid's one tab stop. Right to left: the arrows follow the reading direction.
- **Limit (documented):** the number of group rows is set when the grid is created, from the groups it starts with (the rows are made once). New definitions later with fewer levels leave the lower group rows empty, the column headers stretched over them; with more levels, only the outer ones show; a grid created with no group shows none later.
- Screen readers get `aria-rowspan` / `aria-colspan` on the cell where a span starts, with that cell's row and column indexes; covered places are absent; fillers are hidden from them. Grouped headers are column headers with `aria-colspan`; group rows count in `aria-rowcount`. A full-width cell carries its whole text itself (Chrome ignores `aria-colspan` on such a row). What screen readers actually say is checked by ear (owner's protocol); if one loses the value on covered rows, a hidden repeat is added.
- Right to left: everything is logical (start and end), the travelling label included.
- Keys: moving into a tall cell focuses it (one stop) and remembers the row and column aimed at; the view shows that row, not the cell's first one (Up into a 5 000-row merge does not jump to its top; Page Down inside it scrolls a page at a time). Space acts on the row where the cell starts. Up from the first row still reaches the header.
- Pointer: a click on a tall cell is a click on the row under the pointer (`cellClicked` and `rowClicked` carry that row, with the tall cell's column); Shift+click extends the selection to it. On the focused tall cell, the keys then start from that row: Left and Right go to the clicked row's neighbour, Page Down pages from it, and the view follows from there.
- Full-width rows show the value of the first visible column (the application puts the title there, e.g. a value getter); the cell sticks to the start of the view, after the selection box, as wide as the view.
- A focus left on a covered place by a sort or an update moves to the cell owning it. A focus asked on a covered place (`api.focusCell`, a `focus.set` command) goes to the owning cell at once, in one focus change; the view shows the place asked for (part of the tall cell), and nothing waits to take the focus later.
- The grid tells `missing-feature` once, at creation, when span rules are given without the spans feature; it then ignores them.
