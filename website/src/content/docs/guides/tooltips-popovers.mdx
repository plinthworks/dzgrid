A **tooltip** says a few words about what the pointer rests on, or what the keyboard reaches: it is never clicked. A
**popover** is a bubble that stays: a click opens it, and it can hold a form, a table, even a grid. Both are DzGrid's
own engine, in the grid and anywhere in your page, with the same tones, placements and animations.

## Where tooltips show

Only where you ask for one:

| Where | How |
|---|---|
| A cell | The column's [`tooltip`](../../column-options/tooltip/) function: words, `{ text, tone }`, or HTML (below) |
| A header | The column's [`headerTooltip`](../../column-options/header-tooltip/) |
| A header's buttons (filter, column menu, floating filter toggle) | `tooltips: { header: true }` with the [`tooltips()`](../../features/tooltips/) feature: each says its name |
| Any element of the page | `tooltip(el, options)` from `@dzgrid/dom/tooltip`; in Angular, the `dzTooltip` directive |

A tooltip shows after `delayMs` on hover (500 ms by default), and at once when the keyboard's focus reaches its
element; a click's focus shows nothing, and a finger alone never opens one. The pointer may move onto it; it goes when
the pointer leaves both, after `hideDelayMs` (150 ms). Escape dismisses it without moving the focus. One tooltip is on
screen at a time in a page. While it shows, its element points to it with `aria-describedby`, so a screen reader reads
its words. `tooltips: { cells: false }` silences every column's `tooltip` at once.

The grid's tooltips need no feature for words, tones, presets and HTML. The [`tooltips()`](../../features/tooltips/)
feature adds the rest: the header's buttons, `placement` and `flip`, your own `animate`, a closing that waits for the
end of its fade, and one tooltip at a time with the page's.

## Anywhere in the page

`tooltip(el, options)` gives any element of the page the same tooltip: a toolbar's button, an icon, a field's label.
It returns a handle: `show()`, `hide()`, `update()` with new options, `destroy()` when the element goes. In Angular,
the `DzTooltip` directive from `@dzgrid/angular/tooltip` does the same, and cleans up with its view. The Web
Component has no tooltip of its own for the page: next to a `<dz-grid>`, use the DOM layer's function.

<CodeTabs>
<Fragment slot="ts">

```ts
import { tooltip } from '@dzgrid/dom/tooltip';

const save = document.querySelector<HTMLButtonElement>('#save')!;
const tip = tooltip(save, { content: 'Save the order (Ctrl+S)', placement: 'bottom', tone: 'neutral' });

// New words while it shows, and gone with the button.
tip.update({ content: 'Saved a minute ago' });
tip.destroy();
```

The options: `content`, `delayMs`, `hideDelayMs`, `disabled`, `nonce` (for a strict content security policy: the one
`<style>` it adds carries it), and the look shared with the grid — `tone`, `placement`, `flip`, `animation`,
`reducedMotion`, `animate`, `shape`. The Angular inputs are the same names after `dzTooltip`: `dzTooltipPlacement`,
`dzTooltipTone`, `dzTooltipAnimation`, `dzTooltipDelay`, `dzTooltipFlip`, `dzTooltipReducedMotion`, `dzTooltipAnimate`,
`dzTooltipShape`, `dzTooltipDisabled`, and `dzTooltipContext` / `dzTooltipInputs` for a template or a component.

## Tones

A tone says what kind of message it is. `info` is the dark bubble, the default; `neutral` a raised surface with an
edge; `success`, `warning` and `danger` the palette's colours. Each is readable in light and dark (text at 4.5:1), and
a forced-colours display draws them all in the system's colours with a visible edge. The tone is never the only sign:
let the words say it too.

<TipDemo kind="tones" caption="Rest the pointer on a button, or reach it with Tab." />

Grid-wide, `tooltips: { tone: 'neutral' }`; for one cell, a column's `tooltip` returns `{ text, tone }`; on the page,
the `tone` option or `dzTooltipTone`.

## Placements

`placement` is a side and an alignment: `top`, `bottom`, `start` or `end`, alone (centred on its element) or followed
by `-start` or `-end` (lined up with the element's start or end along the other axis) — 12 placements. `start` and
`end` are the line's: in Arabic or Hebrew they open on the other side, like the rest of the page. Write `left` or
`right` (with the same suffixes) when you mean the screen's side whatever the language. `auto`, the default, opens
below: centred for the page's tooltips, at the cell's start for the grid's.

When the window has no room on that side and has some on the other, the bubble **flips** there; `flip: false` keeps
the side asked. Along the other axis it slides to stay inside the window. Its arrow points at the element's middle and
follows the flip. In the grid, a `placement` (grid-wide, or a column's `{ placement }`) needs the
[`tooltips()`](../../features/tooltips/) feature.

## Animations

A tooltip or a popover opens and closes with one of the presets of `@dzgrid/theme/tooltips.css`:

| Preset | How it opens |
|---|---|
| `fade` | A short fade, the default |
| `scale` | Grows from 96 % with a fade, from the arrow's side |
| `slide` | Moves a few pixels in from the element's side, and follows the flip and the reading direction |
| `pop` | Richer, opt-in: grows from 80 % with a slight overshoot (260 ms) |
| `rise` | Richer, opt-in: 12 px from the element's side, with a long soft deceleration (320 ms) |
| `none` | There at once |

Every preset closes the same short way (100 ms). Only `opacity` and `transform` move: nothing is measured during the
animation. Durations, easings and delays are variables (below).

<TipDemo kind="animations" caption="Each button opens its tooltip with the preset it names." />

**Reduced motion.** When the system asks for reduced motion, or the grid has `motion: false`, nothing moves and nothing
fades: tooltips and popovers appear and go at once. You may ask for a short fade instead, opacity only (120 ms):
`reducedMotion: 'fade'`. A grid with `motion: false` stays instant.

### Your own animation

`animate(el, phase, info)` replaces the presets. It is called once the bubble is placed (`phase` is `'open'`) and
before it is hidden (`'close'`); a closing bubble stays drawn until the promise (or any thenable) you return settles,
and is hidden at once when you return nothing. `info` tells the `side` it opened on (`block-start`, `block-end`,
`inline-start`, `inline-end`), its `tone`, the reading direction `dir`, whether reduced motion is asked (`reduced`: you
decide what that means — nothing at all, or a fade), and the `<path>` of its `shape`. Shown again while it closes, it
is told `'open'` again: stop the closing there. It is never called on a grid with `motion: false`. For the grid's
tooltips, it needs the [`tooltips()`](../../features/tooltips/) feature.

`shape` draws the bubble as your own SVG path instead of a box and an arrow: path data (`d`) in a 100 × 100 box,
stretched over the bubble, filled and edged in the tone's colours. Only path commands and numbers are accepted;
anything else keeps the box. Give the words room inside it (the bubble's padding is yours).

```ts
import { tooltip, type TipAnimate } from '@dzgrid/dom/tooltip';

// Plain keyframes through the Web Animations API, played backwards to close.
const frames: Keyframe[] = [
  { opacity: 0, transform: 'translateY(8px) scale(.85)' },
  { opacity: 1, transform: 'translateY(-2px) scale(1.03)', offset: 0.7 },
  { opacity: 1, transform: 'none' },
];
const bounce: TipAnimate = (el, phase, info) =>
  info.reduced ? undefined : el.animate(phase === 'open' ? frames : [...frames].reverse(), { duration: 220, easing: 'ease-out' }).finished;

tooltip(document.querySelector<HTMLElement>('#harvest')!, {
  content: 'Harvest booked for Tuesday',
  placement: 'top',
  tone: 'success',
  // A speech bubble, its tail at the bottom, toward the element.
  shape: 'M0 0 H100 V80 H60 L50 100 L40 80 H0 Z',
  animate: bounce,
});
```

<TipDemo kind="custom" caption="A speech-bubble shape, opened and closed by plain keyframes through the animate hook." />

**A recipe with GSAP.** `info.shape` is a real `<path>`, so a morphing library can turn one shape into another. Below,
GSAP with its MorphSVG plugin morphs a flat bar into the speech bubble as it opens, and back as it closes, and returns
the timeline (the closing bubble waits for it). GSAP is your application's choice, under its own licence: DzGrid
never depends on it, and the hook works the same with any library or none.

```ts
import { gsap } from 'gsap';
import { MorphSVGPlugin } from 'gsap/MorphSVGPlugin';
import { tooltip, type TipAnimate } from '@dzgrid/dom/tooltip';

gsap.registerPlugin(MorphSVGPlugin);

const BAR = 'M0 40 H100 V60 H60 L50 60 L40 60 H0 Z';
const BUBBLE = 'M0 0 H100 V80 H60 L50 100 L40 80 H0 Z';

const morph: TipAnimate = (el, phase, info) => {
  // Reduced motion: no morph, no movement.
  if (info.reduced || !info.shape) return undefined;
  // The timeline itself: the closing waits for it (a GSAP tween or timeline is a thenable).
  if (phase === 'open') {
    return gsap.timeline()
      .fromTo(info.shape, { morphSVG: BAR }, { morphSVG: BUBBLE, duration: 0.35, ease: 'back.out(1.6)' })
      .fromTo(el, { opacity: 0 }, { opacity: 1, duration: 0.2 }, 0);
  }
  return gsap.timeline()
    .to(info.shape, { morphSVG: BAR, duration: 0.15 })
    .to(el, { opacity: 0, duration: 0.12 }, 0);
};

tooltip(document.querySelector<HTMLElement>('#harvest')!, { content: 'Harvest booked for Tuesday', shape: BUBBLE, animate: morph });
```

## Content

A tooltip shows words, or anything you draw:

- **Words**: a string. In a column, `tooltip` returns one (or null for none), already translated.
- **Words with a tone**: a column's `tooltip` returns `{ text, tone }`.
- **Nodes**: on the page, `content` is a `Node`, or a function called at each opening that returns words, a node, or
  `{ node, gone }` (`gone()` is called when it is taken out). In the grid, a `tooltip` part (the column's `ui.tooltip`)
  draws it.
- **An Angular template or component**: `[dzTooltip]` takes an `<ng-template>` (its `let-` value is
  `dzTooltipContext`) or a component class (its inputs are `dzTooltipInputs`). The view is made at each opening and
  destroyed when it goes.
- **HTML, opt-in**: only when you say so, and cleaned first.

```ts
import { Component } from '@angular/core';
import { DzTooltip } from '@dzgrid/angular/tooltip';

type Hive = { name: string; kilos: number };

@Component({
  selector: 'app-hive',
  imports: [DzTooltip],
  template: `
    <button type="button" [dzTooltip]="card" [dzTooltipContext]="hive" dzTooltipTone="neutral">{{ hive.name }}</button>
    <ng-template #card let-h>
      <strong>{{ h.name }}</strong>
      <p>{{ h.kilos }} kg of honey this year</p>
    </ng-template>
  `,
})
export class HiveButton {
  readonly hive: Hive = { name: 'Clover hill', kilos: 12.5 };
}
```

**HTML.** A tooltip shows HTML given as text only when you opt in. In the grid, pass the cleaner `safeHtml` from
`@dzgrid/dom/tooltip` (so the grid alone never carries it) and let a column's `tooltip` return `{ html }`; without the
cleaner, such a tooltip shows its `text`, or nothing — never the raw markup. On the page, `content: { html }`; in
Angular, `[dzTooltip]="{ html }"`, cleaned by Angular's sanitizer first, then by DzGrid's. The cleaning keeps text and
a fixed list of harmless elements (`b`, `strong`, `em`, `code`, `kbd`, `p`, lists, tables, headings…) with a few
attributes (`class`, `lang`, `dir`, `title`, `colspan`, `rowspan`, `datetime`); scripts, styles, images, links, forms,
frames and SVG go with their content; no event attribute, no `style` and no URL survives. It is parsed in an inert
document, so nothing loads or runs. **The risk that remains is yours**: cleaning stops scripts and loads, not
misleading words — never show HTML a stranger could write without thinking about what it says.

```ts
import { createGrid } from '@dzgrid/dom';
import { safeHtml } from '@dzgrid/dom/tooltip';
import { EN } from '@dzgrid/dom/locales/en';

type Batch = { id: string; batch: string; notes: string };

createGrid<Batch>(document.getElementById('batches')!, {
  rows: [{ id: 'b1', batch: 'Spring honey', notes: '<b>Raw</b>, not heated' }],
  columns: [
    { id: 'batch', header: 'Batch', tooltip: (_v, b) => ({ html: b.notes, text: 'Raw, not heated' }) },
  ],
  getRowId: (b) => b.id,
  messages: EN,
  tooltips: { html: safeHtml },
});
```

## Popovers

`popover(trigger, options)` from `@dzgrid/dom/popover` (in Angular, `[dzPopover]` from `@dzgrid/angular/popover`)
gives an element a bubble that stays: a non-modal dialog, placed and animated like a tooltip.

- **Opening and closing.** A click on the trigger (or Enter, Space) opens it, a second click closes it. Escape closes
  it and puts the focus back on the trigger; a press outside it and its trigger closes it, the focus staying where the
  press put it; the focus leaving it for something outside (Tab past its last control) closes it. Your code opens and
  closes it too: `open()`, `close()`, `toggle()`; `openOn: 'manual'` leaves only the code. `onClose` (Angular:
  `closed`) tells why: `escape`, `outside`, `focus`, `trigger`, `code`, `group` or `gone`.
- **Focus.** On opening, the focus moves to its first control (or the one marked `autofocus`); with nothing focusable
  it stays on the trigger. Tab goes through its controls in order, then on to what follows the trigger in the page;
  Shift+Tab before the first goes back to the trigger. It is not modal: there is no trap.
- **ARIA.** The trigger gets `aria-haspopup="dialog"`, `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls`; the popover is
  `role="dialog"`, named by `label` or by the trigger's own name.
- **Groups and nesting.** One popover is open at a time in a `group` (`'default'` unless given): opening one closes the
  others, except one it was opened from — a popover opened inside another keeps its parent open.
- **Where it sits.** In the browser's top layer, so no ancestor clips it; it stays by its trigger when the page
  scrolls or the window resizes, and closes when its trigger leaves the page or the window.
- **In a grid's cell.** A trigger drawn in a cell (a part, an Angular cell component) works the same; the popover
  closes when its row is recycled to show another row.

Its default look is a raised surface with an edge and a shadow (`--dzg-popover-*`); `tone`, `animation`, `placement`,
`flip`, `shape` and `animate` are the tooltip's.

## The style sheet

The tones, the arrow on four sides, the animations, the shapes and the popover's look are an opt-in sheet, loaded after
`dzgrid.css`:

```ts
import '@dzgrid/theme/dzgrid.css';
import '@dzgrid/theme/tooltips.css';
```

With an unlayered reset (Tailwind 3's preflight, Bootstrap's Reboot), load `@dzgrid/theme/tooltips.unlayered.css`
instead. Without the sheet a tooltip keeps the grid's plain dark bubble and appears without animation, and a popover is
a plain surface. Every colour, size, duration and easing is a variable, listed in
[the table of variables](../theming-rtl/#every-variable) (`--dzg-tooltip-*`, `--dzg-popover-*`):

```css
:root {
  --dzg-tooltip-open-duration: 200ms;
  --dzg-tooltip-pop-scale: 0.7;
  --dzg-popover-radius: 0.75rem;
}
```

### Light and dark

Tooltips and popovers follow the colour scheme of their trigger's context, as the grid does: the system's by default,
`data-theme="light"` or `data-theme="dark"` on any ancestor (or `--tz-color-scheme`) to force one. Tailwind's `.dark`
class is mapped by `@dzgrid/theme/tailwind.css` and `tailwind3.css`. Your own dark class, mapped once:

```css
.my-dark { --tz-color-scheme: dark; }
```

A page switching while a bubble is open updates it at once; a wrapper of its own switching updates it at its next
opening. The page's `color-scheme` property alone is not read (the grid does not read it either): set
`--tz-color-scheme` beside it.
